A dividend is part of a company's profit that it pays to the people who own its shares.

{{definition}}

{{lead0}}{{lead1}}{{lead2}}

Key takeaways

  • Dividends are usually paid as a fixed amount per share.
  • Not every company pays them, and they can be cut or stopped.
  • Dividend yield compares the yearly payout with the share price.
  • {{k}}

How dividends work

When a company makes a profit, it can keep the money to grow, or share some with its owners. If you own shares, you are one of those owners.

  1. 1The company's board decides how much to pay per share.
  2. 2It sets a cut-off date. You must own the shares before it to qualify.
  3. 3The money is paid to shareholders, usually into a bank account.

African example

Fictional company

Adire Foods Plc, listed in Lagos, pays ₦1.50 for every share.

Shares you own200
Dividend per share₦1.50
You receive₦300
200 × ₦1.50 = ₦300, before any tax.

Dividend yield

Yield shows how big the payout is compared with the share price.

Dividend ÷ Share price = Yield ₦1.50 ÷ ₦30 = 5% If one Adire Foods share costs ₦30
